What we're proposing

A step-by-step guide for individuals in place of the Retail Questions and Answers page, with jargon explained where it appears, a short eligibility check for the Purchase and Sale Service and a tool to value a holding. The terms and conditions are linked once, at the point they matter.

Why

The Retail Investors page is one paragraph and ten links. The Q&A behind it is a single long page in ten sections, which opens by saying it isn't the terms and conditions. Along the way it cites the Money Laundering Regulations 2007, replaced in 2017, and still has a drafting bracket in one answer, "[reside in the UK and that]". Retail investors are a small audience for the DMO but probably the least expert one, and GOV.UK content guidance is to write for them rather than in FAQs.

Service details are taken from the current Q&A. They should be checked with the DMO and Computershare before any of this is used.

Step by step

Buy and sell gilts as an individual

How to buy gilts, the government bonds the DMO sells on behalf of HM Treasury, and how to sell them again.

The DMO can't give investment advice. If you're not sure whether gilts are right for you, speak to a regulated financial adviser.

  1. Understand what a gilt is

    A gilt is a loan to the government. You get a fixed amount of interest, called the coupon, twice a year, and your money back on the redemption date.

    What does "4⅞% Treasury Gilt 2036" mean?
    It pays interest of £4.875 a year for every £100 of the gilt you hold, in two payments of about £2.44, and repays £100 for each £100 you hold in 2036. The £100 is called the nominal amount. The price you pay to buy it can be more or less than £100.
    What is an index-linked gilt?
    Interest and the amount repaid go up and down in line with inflation, measured by the Retail Prices Index.

    What gilts are and how they work

  2. Choose how to buy

    There are 3 ways to buy gilts.

    • Through a stockbroker or investment platform.
    • Through your bank's share-dealing service.
    • Through the DMO's Purchase and Sale Service, run for the DMO by Computershare. It's a postal service for UK residents, and you need to join the Approved Group first.

    Check if you can use the Purchase and Sale Service

    ContentWhether to show the service's commission rates here, or link to Computershare's, depends on who maintains them. See question Q4.

  3. Join the Approved Group

    Only if you're using the Purchase and Sale Service to buy. It's free. You'll need to prove your identity and where your money comes from, to meet the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017.

    Apply to join the Approved Group (on the Computershare website)

  4. Send your instruction and payment

    Fill in a Gilt Stock Dealing Form and send it to Computershare by email as a clear scanned PDF, or by post.

    • Instructions received by 10am on a dealing day are dealt that day.
    • For more than £1,000 on a dealing day you must pay electronically. For £1,000 or less you can also pay by cheque.
    • You can't set a price. The service buys at the market price when it deals.
    What is a dealing day?
    Any business day when the London Stock Exchange is open.

  5. Get your gilt certificate

    Settlement is normally 3 business days after your form is accepted, or later if you pay by cheque. Computershare sends your certificate about 4 business days after the purchase.

  6. Receive interest, and sell when you want to

    Interest is paid twice a year into your bank account. You can sell at any time through the same routes; you don't need to be in the Approved Group to sell through the Purchase and Sale Service, but you do need to live in the UK.
